Zion hikes, from riverside walks to permit-only ridges

Distances, climbs, and what to know before each trail. Pick by effort and conditions, not by fame.

Check conditions before every hike. Trail closures, permit rules, and river flow change. Figures on this page are approximate and for planning only. Confirm current details at nps.gov/zion.

Riverside Walk

Round trip
2.2 mi
Climb
about 60 ft
Effort
Easy

A paved path along the Virgin River that ends where The Narrows begins. It suits any fitness level, and the canyon walls give shade on hot afternoons. In busy months you reach it by shuttle.

Lower Emerald Pool

Round trip
1.2 mi
Climb
about 70 ft
Effort
Easy

A short walk to a pool beneath a seasonal waterfall. Flow is strongest after rain or snowmelt and can fade to a trickle in dry months.

The Narrows (bottom-up)

Round trip
up to 9.4 mi
Climb
minimal
Effort
Strenuous

You walk in the Virgin River between walls up to about 1,000 feet tall. The water is cold, the bottom is uneven, and flash floods are a real risk, so check the forecast and the ranger report. Most visitors rent canyon shoes and a walking stick in Springdale.

Angels Landing

Round trip
5.4 mi
Climb
about 1,500 ft
Effort
Strenuous

Steep switchbacks climb to Scout Lookout, then a narrow ridge with chains and drop-offs. A permit, awarded by lottery on Recreation.gov, is required beyond Scout Lookout. If heights make you uneasy, Scout Lookout has strong views without the exposed section.

Observation Point (East Mesa)

Round trip
6.7 mi
Climb
about 700 ft
Effort
Moderate

Reaches a rim overlook high above Zion Canyon. This route starts on the east side and is reached by road from outside the main canyon. Access has changed over the years, so confirm which route is open.

Taylor Creek Trail (Kolob Canyons)

Round trip
5.0 mi
Climb
about 450 ft
Effort
Moderate

In the park's quieter northwest corner, this trail follows a creek past old homestead cabins to Double Arch Alcove. Kolob Canyons has its own entrance off Interstate 15.
